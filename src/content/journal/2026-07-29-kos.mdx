Kos is a beautiful Dodecanese island just off the coast of Turkey. It is perhaps best known for being the birthplace of Hippocrates, the “Father of Medicine.” In Kos, you can see the plane tree that Hippocrates supposedly taught his pupils beneath, as well as another statue of him wielding the rod of Asclepius. Other sights include the Sanctuary of Hercules, located in the Ancient Agora, which is nearby the bustling harbor of pirate ships and other attractions.

<PhotoPlaceholder caption="[PLACEHOLDER: the plane tree of Hippocrates]" />

However, perhaps the most well-known archaeological site is the Asclepieion of Kos, built on three huge terraces overlooking the Turkish coast. One of the three major Asclepieia in the Ancient Greek world, it contains baths, various stoa, temples from various eras, and of course an Enkoimeterion (another word for Abaton). Funnily, there was a monument of Gaius Stertinius Xenophon, a Koan physician who allegedly poisoned the Roman emperor Claudius.
