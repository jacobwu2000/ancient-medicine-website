There is much to say about Athens, the capital of Greece and once the dominant city-state in the region. There is also much to see there, but I will only go over some of the relevant sites. Adjacent to the Acropolis is the Asclepieion, just west of the Theater of Dionysus. It was founded in the 5th century BC during the Peloponnesian War by an Acharnian citizen named Telechamus, to whom a monument is dedicated. The Doric Stoa, which like the Abaton was where incubation occurred, was integrated into the Sacred Spring and the Sacred Bothros, a sacrificial pit for heroes.

Another site in Athens is the National Archaeological Museum, which holds a number of gems such as the famed Mask of Agamemnon and the Antikythera Mechanism. One of the museum rooms is specifically dedicated to artifacts from the Asclepieion at Epidaurus, containing sculptures from the pediment of the temple depicting the Amazonomachy and the Sack of Troy. Other rooms hold more statues from the sanctuary, including ones of the god Asclepius himself and his daughter Hygeia.

<PhotoPlaceholder caption="[PLACEHOLDER: the Epidaurus room, National Archaeological Museum]" />

Other medical-related items at the museum include an array of medical instruments, including needles, hooks, various probes, forceps, knives, and scalpels.
