<PersonalObservations>
The archaeological site of Ancient Corinth is well-known for sites like the monumental Temple of Apollo, the Fountain of Glauke, and the Roman Agora where Paul of Tarsus preached. Not visitable at the archaeological site, however, is the Asclepieion, of which only the foundation trenches are preserved. The museum at the archaeological site contains a section devoted to the Asclepieion which displays some inscriptions and terracotta body parts which may have been used as votive offerings. The Asclepieion was destroyed by the Roman General Lucius Mummius, who conquered Corinth, though it was later rebuilt. In the 6th and 7th centuries, the temple was converted into a site for Christian burials.
